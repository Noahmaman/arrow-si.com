“We asked ChatGPT and it recommended us!” is the most common, and least useful, AI visibility report. Ask again tomorrow, from another city, with slightly different wording, and you may get a different answer. Measurement has to be designed for that variability.

Here’s a method that holds up.

## Step 1: Write a buyer-question panel

Start from what buyers actually ask, not from your keyword list. Interview sales, read support tickets and call notes, and look at the questions people type into search. Then group them by intent:

| Intent group | Example question | What it tests |
|---|---|---|
| **Category discovery** | “Best project management tool for architecture firms” | Do you make the shortlist? |
| **Problem diagnosis** | “Why do our client projects keep running over budget?” | Are you associated with the problem you solve? |
| **Comparison** | “Your Brand vs Competitor A for small teams” | Is the comparison fair and accurate? |
| **Alternatives** | “Alternatives to Competitor A” | Are you named when buyers look to switch? |
| **Brand facts** | “How much does Your Brand cost?” | Are your facts correct and is your site the source? |

Aim for **20–50 questions**. Include unbranded questions: most of your opportunity is where buyers don’t yet know your name. Arrow Answers has a detailed guide to [building a buyer-question tracking panel](https://answers.arrow-ai.us/how-to-build-a-buyer-question-tracking-panel/).

**Freeze the panel.** If you change questions every month, you can’t compare months.

## Step 2: Choose engines and a test protocol

Pick the two to four engines your buyers actually use. For most B2B and B2C brands: ChatGPT, Google AI Overviews / AI Mode, Gemini and Perplexity; add Copilot for Microsoft-heavy audiences and Claude for technical ones.

Then write down a protocol and never deviate:

- **Session:** logged-out or a fresh account, memory and personalization off.
- **Location and language:** fixed (use a consistent location if your market is local).
- **Mode:** note whether web search was on (for example, ChatGPT search vs. a no-browsing answer).
- **Wording:** paste the exact question; no follow-ups unless they are part of the panel.
- **Repetition:** run each question more than once per cycle if your budget allows, to estimate variability.

## Step 3: Capture evidence, not impressions

For every run, save:

- the **full answer text** (and a screenshot for the record);
- **engine, mode, date, time, location**;
- every **cited URL** and its domain;
- the **position** of your brand if it appears in a list.

This archive is what makes the work defensible. When a stakeholder asks “did we really improve?”, you can show the answers.

## Step 4: Score four signals

Score each answer separately for:

1. **Mentioned**: is the brand named?
2. **Own URL cited**: is one of your pages linked as a source?
3. **Recommended**: is the brand suggested as a top choice for the stated need? (Define this strictly.)
4. **Accurate**: is everything said about you correct?

Why separate? Because each one needs different work: see [mentions, citations and recommendations](/blog/mentions-citations-recommendations/).

## Step 5: Compute rates and share of voice

Roll the scores up into rates per engine and per intent group:

- **Mention rate** = answers mentioning you ÷ total answers
- **Own citation rate** = answers citing your URL ÷ answers that show sources
- **Recommendation rate** = answers recommending you ÷ total answers
- **Accuracy rate** = answers with no errors about you ÷ answers mentioning you
- **Share of voice** = your mentions ÷ all brand mentions in the competitive set

Share of voice deserves its own method: see [AI share of voice](/blog/ai-share-of-voice/).

If leadership wants one number, build a weighted **AI visibility score** (for example 40% recommendation, 30% mention, 20% own citation, 10% accuracy), but publish the weights, and keep the rates visible underneath.

## Step 6: Report the trend and the evidence

A good monthly report fits on one page:

- the rates and share of voice, with the previous period;
- the **biggest wins and losses**, each linked to a saved answer;
- the **top cited domains** for your category (they tell you where to earn evidence);
- **accuracy issues** found and their status;
- the next three actions.

Pair it with [AI referral traffic](/blog/track-ai-referral-traffic-ga4/) from your analytics, so you can connect visibility to visits and leads.
