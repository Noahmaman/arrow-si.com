The Arrow film has a chapter called **“Build synthetic authority.”** The phrase is deliberate: in AI answers, authority isn’t something you declare. It’s something that’s **synthesized** from what many sources say about you. Your website is one voice. The engine is listening to all of them.

## Why your own site isn’t enough

When an AI engine recommends a provider, it is trying to give the buyer a safe answer. A company saying “we’re the best” is weak evidence. Independent sources describing the company — with specifics — are strong evidence.

That’s why, when you check what an engine cites alongside a recommendation, you often see review platforms, comparison articles, industry publications and community threads rather than the brand’s own homepage.

Your own site still matters enormously — it’s where engines confirm facts and where citations of your pages come from (see [content AI engines cite](/blog/content-ai-engines-cite/)). But **recommendations** usually need corroboration.

## The six types of third-party sources

| Source type | Examples | What it proves |
|---|---|---|
| **Review platforms** | Category review sites, app marketplaces, Google reviews | Real customers’ experience |
| **Comparisons & “best of”** | Editorial roundups, analyst lists | Your place among alternatives |
| **Industry publications** | Trade media, association sites | Expertise and relevance |
| **Communities** | Forums, Q&A sites, discussion threads | Unprompted peer opinion |
| **Video** | Reviews, tutorials, talks | Demonstrated use |
| **Directories & partners** | Associations, integration directories, partner pages | Existence, category, relationships |

## Step 1 — Find the sources that shape *your* answers

Don’t chase generic lists of “top AI sources.” Measure your own:

1. Run your [buyer-question panel](/blog/how-to-measure-ai-visibility/) on the engines that show sources.
2. Export every **cited domain** from every answer.
3. Count frequency per intent group, and note which domains appear when **competitors are recommended**.

The result is a ranked target list: the 10–20 domains that most influence AI answers in your category. That list is your PR and partnerships roadmap.

## Step 2 — Check what they say about you

For each target domain:

- Are you **present** at all?
- Is the description **accurate and current**?
- Is it **specific** — who you serve, what you do, results — or generic?
- Is your **category** right?

Fix inaccuracies first; they spread into answers.

## Step 3 — Earn presence, with proof

- **Reviews:** ask happy customers systematically, on the platforms that appear in your answers. Make it easy; never incentivise in ways the platform prohibits.
- **Comparisons:** contact authors of roundups with genuinely useful information — a clear description, pricing, differentiators, a customer example. Make their job easier.
- **Expert contributions:** offer data, commentary or case studies to industry publications. Original data is especially citable.
- **Communities:** participate as yourself, transparently, where you can genuinely help. Don’t astroturf.
- **Partners:** get listed — accurately — in integration directories, associations and partner pages.

## Step 4 — Give everyone the same story

Every source should describe you the same way. Prepare a press kit with your **canonical description**, logo, founding facts, pricing approach and proof points — the same one you use for [entity clarity](/blog/entity-clarity-ai-search/). Consistency across independent sources is what makes an engine confident.

## What not to do

- **Fake reviews** or review gating.
- **Undisclosed paid placements** dressed as editorial.
- **Mass-produced guest posts** and link schemes.
- **Sock-puppet community posts.**

Search engines treat these as spam, platforms remove them, and buyers see through them. The short-term bump isn’t worth the trust you lose.

## Measure the effect

Track three numbers over time: how many of your target domains mention you accurately, your **recommendation share** in the answers where those domains are cited (see [AI share of voice](/blog/ai-share-of-voice/)), and the accuracy of what engines say about you.
