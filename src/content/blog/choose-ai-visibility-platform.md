The AI visibility market went from zero to dozens of tools in two years. Dashboards look alike, every vendor has a “visibility score”, and demos always show the brand winning. Here’s how to cut through it.

## First, decide what you’re buying

There are three models. Many teams end up combining two.

| Model | What you get | Best when |
|---|---|---|
| **DIY** | Spreadsheet, manual runs, your time | You’re starting, with 20–30 questions and 2–3 engines |
| **Tracking software** | Automated runs, dashboards, alerts | You have an in-house team to act on findings |
| **Managed GEO** | Measurement *plus* the fixes, done for you | You need results and don’t have the team or time |

A tracker tells you what’s wrong. Someone still has to fix crawler access, rewrite pages, align entity data and earn third-party evidence. If nobody on your team owns that work, software alone becomes an expensive way to watch a problem.

## The checklist

### 1. Coverage

- Which engines? (ChatGPT, Google AI Overviews and AI Mode, Gemini, Perplexity, Claude, Copilot…)
- Which **modes**? Web search on/off, logged-out, specific models?
- Which **locations and languages**?
- Does it match where **your** buyers ask?

### 2. Method

- How are questions run: through official APIs, browser sessions, or both? Do results match what a user sees?
- Are questions **repeated** to account for variability?
- Can you **freeze** your panel and competitor set?
- Is the protocol documented?

### 3. Evidence

This is non-negotiable. For every data point you should be able to see:

- the **full answer text**;
- **engine, mode, date, location**;
- every **cited URL**.

A score you can’t trace back to answers isn’t evidence. See [how to measure AI visibility](/blog/how-to-measure-ai-visibility/).

### 4. Definitions

- How does the tool define a **mention**, a **citation** and a **recommendation**? Are they reported separately?
- Does it check **accuracy** of what’s said about you?
- How is **share of voice** calculated, which unit, which weights? See [AI share of voice](/blog/ai-share-of-voice/).

### 5. Actionability

- Does it show **which sources** are cited when competitors win?
- Does it flag **technical blockers** (crawler access, indexing)?
- Does it connect to **analytics or CRM** for AI referral traffic and leads?
- Does it recommend specific fixes, and are those fixes sensible?

### 6. Data ownership

- Can you **export** questions, answers and scores?
- If you switch, can you keep your **baseline**? Arrow Answers covers [switching tools without losing your baseline](https://answers.arrow-ai.us/how-to-switch-ai-visibility-tools/).

### 7. Cost

- Pricing per question, per engine, per run or per seat?
- What’s the **total** for your real panel at your real frequency?
- What does the **work** cost on top: internal time or agency fees?

## Run a two-week trial on your own questions

Never decide from the vendor’s demo prompts. Give each shortlisted option:

1. your **frozen panel** of 20–30 buyer questions;
2. your **competitor set**;
3. your **engines and location**.

Then compare their outputs with a few manual runs you do yourself. Large unexplained gaps are a red flag about method.

## Questions to ask every vendor

- “Show me the raw answers behind this score.”
- “How do you handle answer variability?”
- “Which engines do you query, and how?”
- “What happens to my data if I leave?”
- “What does your platform **not** do?”
- For managed services: “Who approves changes to our site?” and “What exactly counts as a monthly improvement?”
