When an AI engine writes an answer, it doesn’t quote your page. It quotes a **passage**: a definition, a price, a comparison row, a number. Content that gets cited is content where those passages are easy to find, easy to trust and easy to lift without losing meaning.

Here’s how to write it.

## 1. Lead with the answer

The first two sentences of a page, and of every section, should answer the question directly. No warm-up, no history lesson.

**Weak:** *“In today’s fast-moving digital landscape, many businesses wonder about pricing…”*

**Strong:** *“Arrow GEO Start costs $990 one-time plus $490 per month. Setup takes three to five weeks once access is received.”*

This is the single highest-leverage change you can make. Every article in this journal opens with a “short answer” box for exactly this reason.

## 2. Make every section stand alone

Engines extract chunks. A section that depends on three earlier paragraphs to make sense is hard to use.

- Use **headings that are questions or clear statements** (“How much does it cost?”, “Who it’s for”).
- Repeat the **subject** instead of pronouns: “Arrow GEO includes…” not “It includes…”.
- Keep each section **complete**: claim, evidence, limits.

## 3. Be specific: numbers, names, dates

Vague claims can’t be verified, so they’re weak evidence. Specific claims can.

| Vague | Specific (example) |
|---|---|
| “Fast setup” | “Setup in 3–5 weeks after access is received” |
| “Trusted by leading brands” | “Used by 40 clinics in Florida, including [named example]” |
| “Affordable pricing” | “From $490/month, billed monthly, no annual prepayment” |
| “Great results” | “Cited in 18 of 30 tracked ChatGPT answers after 90 days” |

The GEO research paper by Aggarwal et al. found that adding **statistics, quotations and citations** to source content increased its visibility in generated answers in their benchmark. Only use numbers you can source: invented precision is worse than none.

## 4. Show your evidence

Link to the source of every important claim: your case studies, third-party reviews, official documentation, data. Name the author and their expertise. Show when the page was last updated. These are the same signals Google describes in its guidance on helpful, reliable, people-first content, and they help engines decide that your page is a safe source to cite.

## 5. Build pages around decisions, not keywords

Buyers ask AI to help them **decide**. The pages that get cited answer decision questions:

- **Pricing:** what it costs, what’s included, what’s extra.
- **Fit:** who it’s for, and who it’s not for.
- **Comparison:** you vs. alternatives, against clear criteria.
- **Process:** what happens after I sign, how long it takes.
- **Proof:** results, examples, reviews.
- **Problems:** the symptoms you solve, in the buyer’s words.

Start with the questions that block a real purchase. Arrow Answers has a method for [choosing which questions to cover first](https://answers.arrow-ai.us/how-to-choose-questions-for-an-answer-hub/).

## 6. Use structure engines can parse

- **Tables** for comparisons, specs and pricing tiers.
- **Numbered steps** for processes.
- **Short lists** for criteria.
- **FAQ sections** for discrete follow-up questions (with matching FAQPage markup where appropriate: see [structured data for AI visibility](/blog/schema-markup-ai-visibility/)).
- **Text, not images**, for anything important. A price in a PNG is a price no crawler can read.

## 7. Be honest about limits

Pages that state what they *don’t* do, where the evidence is thin or what depends on the situation read as more trustworthy, to people and to engines. “We don’t serve companies under 10 employees” is a useful fact. “Results vary; here’s what affects them” beats a guarantee nobody believes.

## 8. Keep one canonical page per fact

If your price appears on six pages and you update four, engines will find the other two. Put each core fact on one canonical page and link to it. This is also the foundation of [entity clarity](/blog/entity-clarity-ai-search/).

## A page template that works

1. **Title** that states the question or decision.
2. **Short answer** (40–60 words).
3. **Key facts** table or list.
4. **Sections** answering the follow-up questions, each self-contained.
5. **Evidence**: examples, data, sources.
6. **Limits and alternatives**.
7. **FAQ** for remaining questions.
8. **Author, date, next step.**

## Then measure

Publishing is a hypothesis. Re-run your [buyer-question panel](/blog/how-to-measure-ai-visibility/) on the questions the page targets, and check whether the page gets cited. If it doesn’t after it has been recrawled, look at what *does* get cited and what it says that yours doesn’t.
